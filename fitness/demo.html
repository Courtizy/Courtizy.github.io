      How each rating is decided

      What each rating measures comes from your evidence reference. The cut-offs between Strong, Fair and Weak are judgment calls, because the research doesn't publish thresholds for whole programs.

      Build muscle
      Evidence Direct sets per muscle between RP's 2024 MEV and MRV landmarks (RP muscle guides, via your evidence reference). Cut-off Strong = 11+ of 13 muscles in range, Fair = 9–10.
      Strength
      Evidence Strength needs heavy, specific work (80%+ of 1RM); gains flatten around 5 sets a week per lift (Pelland meta-regression), and about 2 heavy sets per session (Remmert/Pelland preprint). Cut-off Strong = 4+ heavy sets a week across the tested lifts, Fair = 2–4. Lenient next to the per-lift figure.
      Conditioning
      Evidence Cardio doesn't meaningfully blunt size or strength, mainly power, and mostly when it shares a session with lifting (concurrent-training section). No research gives a weekly dose for this goal. Cut-off Strong = 4+ sessions a week with a metcon, EMOM, AMRAP or finisher, Fair = 2–4.
      Fits 5×60
      Basis Your schedule, not research. Cut-off 5.5 or fewer training days, and at most 35% of sessions estimated over 60 minutes (Fair up to 50%). The time estimate is rough: 8 min warm-up plus 2 min per set.
      Lean out
      Evidence Heavy work is the main signal to keep muscle in a deficit, and less volume is needed to maintain than to build (RP maintenance volume below MEV). Cut-off Fair or better on strength, conditioning and time all at once. Ties are broken by shorter sessions and more conditioning, weighted about equally (conditioning adds modest extra fat loss on top of diet in trials such as Willis et al. 2012), then by heavy sets. The weights are judgment.

      Weight, intake and expenditure

      Is weight moving at the target rate?

      MacroFactor doesn't measure expenditure. It infers it from logged calories and the change in trend weight, so unlogged or partly logged days pull the estimate down. Covers up to the last two years.

      Trend weightScale weigh-in MacroFactor expenditure estimateCalorie target Logged dayWeekday loggedFri–Sun logged

      Can I trust the calorie numbers?

      Which days get logged. Missed days, especially weekends, make intake look lower than it was and drag MacroFactor's expenditure estimate down.

      Expenditure explorer

      Sizes the gap in MacroFactor's estimate. Pick a stretch of time, then set what you think you ate on the days you didn't log. Energy balance then gives the expenditure that fits your trend-weight change. It uses about 3,500 kcal per pound, a common rule of thumb that works over weeks but not day to day.

      Research context: in Nedeltcheva et al. (2010), dieters on 5.5 hours in bed lost as much weight as on 8.5 hours, but less of it was fat. Al Khatib et al. (2017) found short sleep adds about 385 kcal a day of intake.

      Before test day

        Test-week rules

        • Nothing in Block 2 or the runs is cut. Reorder that week's days (swap them on the calendar) so the test lands on a rest day.
        • Both leg days and the interval run go at least 72 hours before the test; intervals 4–5 days out is ideal.
        • Skip that week's PT extras, do Core B only, and nothing extra in the 72 hours before. Rides drop to 20 min easy.
        • Add your booked test day with + and pick PT test. The calendar then flags anything that breaks these rules.

          Waist, push-ups and core

          Log a baseline now and a re-test every couple of weeks. Score each against the current AFPC chart for your age bracket; the dashboard keeps the numbers and the trend, not the points.

            Counting rules: every drop in a dropset is a full set. Warm-up sets on the lifts, 1RM ramp-up sets and conditioning (metcons, EMOMs, AMRAPs, tests, bike/row/run) are excluded. Warm-up moves from the day notes that train a muscle (squats, lunges, push-ups, planks, band pull-aparts) count as 1 set each; cardio and mobility drills in the warm-up don't. In the effort-weighted view they count as a quarter set. Core work is included. The muscle mapping is a judgment call, not Liftosaur's data. Lunges, split squats and step-ups count fully toward both quads and glutes, which pushes those two up. RP landmarks are averages for intermediate lifters, taken from a secondary summary.
